Conceptual Research and Decision Science

Composite Load Regulation Model (CLRM)

A conceptual framework examining how cognitive regulatory demand, ambiguity-processing capacity, and regulatory balance may shape interpretation and decision making under increasing cognitive load.

Overview

CLRM is a developing theoretical framework focused on the relationship between cognitive demand and the capacity available to process ambiguity, complexity, and competing interpretations.

The model proposes that interpretive flexibility may change when regulatory demands increase relative to available processing capacity. Rather than treating this shift primarily as a failure of reasoning, CLRM examines whether some forms of interpretive contraction may function as adaptive regulatory responses under cognitive pressure.

Research Problem

Existing explanations of judgment and belief change often emphasize motivation, bias, traits, or information quality. CLRM investigates whether changes in interpretation may also be explained by the balance between regulatory demand and available ambiguity-processing capacity.

Central question:

How does the relationship between cognitive regulatory demand and ambiguity-processing capacity affect interpretive flexibility under increasing cognitive load?

Core Model

The current framework centers on three related elements:

  1. Cognitive Regulatory Load (CRL) represents the demand placed on cognitive resources by internal and external conditions.
  2. Ambiguity Processing Capacity (APC) represents the capacity available to sustain uncertainty, complexity, and competing interpretations.
  3. Interpretive Contraction describes a proposed narrowing of interpretive range when regulatory demand exceeds available processing capacity.

Research Development

The model developed through repeated comparison of resource-based, motivational, and interpretive explanations of decision behavior. Over time, the framework shifted toward a resource-regulation account that treats changing interpretive flexibility as a potentially adaptive response to cognitive conditions.

The current version is intentionally narrower than earlier formulations. Related work in belief architecture, cognitive ecology, and control effectiveness is being developed separately and connected only where a precise theoretical relationship is established.

Peer-Review Status

An initial CLRM manuscript received external journal peer review. The reviewer characterized the work as a promising and coherent conceptual framework while identifying substantial development needs in empirical grounding, operational precision, novelty differentiation, and positioning against competing theories.

The project is therefore in active post-review refinement rather than being presented as a validated empirical model.

Current Research Questions

  • How can CRL and APC be operationalized using observable indicators?
  • What empirical findings would support or falsify the proposed model?
  • What does CLRM explain that existing theories do not explain as well?
  • How should CLRM be positioned relative to cognitive load theory, bounded rationality, ambiguity tolerance, and related frameworks?
  • What experimental or observational designs could test the model?

Methods & Skills Demonstrated

  • Conceptual model development
  • Interdisciplinary literature synthesis
  • Theoretical comparison and differentiation
  • Hypothesis development
  • Peer-review response and research revision
  • Operationalization and empirical-design planning

Current Status

CLRM is a developing conceptual framework undergoing substantial post-peer-review refinement. Current work focuses on operationalization, falsifiability, theoretical distinctiveness, and empirical test design before manuscript revision and future resubmission.
