## Research

My research generally begins with an observed problem or question and works backward into the processes and mechanisms that may produce it.

Recurring questions include:

- Why does this outcome occur?
- What process produces it?
- What mechanism operates inside that process?
- Under what conditions does it change or fail?
- Does a proposed explanation or intervention actually work?
- What did the investigation reveal that was not part of the original question?
- What question should come next?

## Research Principles

Several principles guide the work presented here:

**Conceptual development is not empirical validation.**  
A framework can be well developed conceptually while still requiring direct empirical support.

**Evidence should be capable of challenging an idea.**  
Research should examine competing explanations, contradictory evidence, and boundary conditions rather than collecting only supportive sources.

**Failure can produce useful information.**  
An unsuccessful hypothesis or intervention may reveal an incorrect assumption, expose another mechanism, establish a boundary condition, or create a new research question.

**Related ideas should not be forced together.**  
Projects may share mechanisms, methods, or questions without necessarily belonging to the same theory.

**Effectiveness matters.**  
Understanding why something happens is often only the beginning. When research produces an intervention, process, control, or other application, the next question is whether it actually works.
