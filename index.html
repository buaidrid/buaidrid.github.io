Understanding Why Systems Work, Fail, and Change

Research and analysis focused on the processes behind outcomes: how people make decisions, how controls perform under real conditions, how failures develop, and how systems can be designed to work more effectively.

Systems, Processes, and the Question Behind Them

My work crosses several areas that initially appear separate: psychology, data, banking, fraud, operational controls, organizational systems, and independent research.

The connection is process analysis.

I am interested in understanding what produces an outcome, what happens inside the process, where failure or distortion can occur, and whether the system actually works under the conditions in which people use it.

A recurring question:

If we understand why an outcome occurs, what can we do with that understanding, and what question should we ask next?

How I Approach Problems

Understand the Process

Identify the outcome, reconstruct the process producing it, and examine the mechanisms, assumptions, inputs, and conditions operating inside the system.

Challenge the Explanation

Look for competing explanations, missing variables, evidence gaps, failure conditions, and reasons an apparently successful model may not hold under different circumstances.

Follow What Happens Next

Ask what can be done with the explanation, whether an intervention actually works, what failure reveals, and what new question becomes possible because of the result.
